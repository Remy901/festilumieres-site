Privacy policy

Last updated: November 9, 2026 · Version française

In short: FestiLumieres collects no personal data. No account, no ads, no analytics, no server. Everything happens on your device and on your home Wi-Fi.

Your lights and the local network

To find and control your smart lights (Philips Hue, WiZ, LIFX, Govee, Nanoleaf), FestiLumieres talks to them directly over your Wi-Fi network. This traffic stays inside your home: it goes through no server.

One exception: to find a Philips Hue bridge, FestiLumieres also asks Philips' public service (discovery.meethue.com), which returns the address of the Hue bridges on your network. Like any website, this service sees your connection's IP address. No other information is sent to it.

The microphone

The microphone is only used for microphone mode, when you ask for it: FestiLumieres listens to the music playing in the room so your lights react to its beat. Sound is analysed live on your device, then immediately discarded. Nothing is recorded, nothing is sent over the Internet. The microphone stops as soon as you tap STOP or leave the app.

Your music

With « My music », FestiLumieres reads the track you choose to find its rhythm, directly on your device. The file is neither copied nor sent.

Data kept on your device

FestiLumieres only keeps what it needs to find your lights again: the pairing keys of your Hue bridges and Nanoleaf panels, the list of lights that take part in the show, and your settings (reduced flashing, warning already read). This information never leaves your device and disappears if you delete the app.

Contacting us

In the app, Settings > « Support & contact » opens this site's support page in your browser. This site is hosted by GitHub Pages: visiting it falls under GitHub's privacy policy, like any website.

If you email us, we only use your address to reply to you, and never share it.

Children

As FestiLumieres collects no personal data, the whole family can use it. Some scenes produce flashing light: a warning is shown on first launch, and « reduced flashing » mode softens them.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new version will be published on this page, with its update date.
